A new discovery every week — sometimes a piece everybody knows, sometimes something wonderful you've never heard. A little to read, and always something to watch.

This week Week of September 8, 2026

Saint-Saëns — The Swan

A cello melody floating over rippling piano — one of the most beloved pieces ever written for the instrument. Listen for how the two hands of the piano paint the water while the cello simply sings above it. Ask your child: is the swan moving quickly or slowly? What do they hear underneath?

Week of September 1, 2026

Florence Price — Adoration

A short, warm piece by a composer whose music sat unplayed for decades before being rediscovered. It works beautifully on violin, viola or cello, and many of our students grow into it around Book 4.

Recently answered

My daughter rushes through her review pieces. How do I slow her down without a fight?

Try giving the review a job: play it for a stuffed audience, play it as slowly as a turtle, or play it while you clap the beat. When review has a game attached to it, speed stops being the point.

Answered by an OSM teacher

How much daily listening is enough?

Little and often beats one long session. Breakfast and the car ride to school are usually enough to cover the current piece plus the next one.
